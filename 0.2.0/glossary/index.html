    Glossary¶

    The terms used across these pages, in plain words. Throughout the site, abbreviations with a dotted underline show their meaning when you hover over them.

    The balance sheet and accounting¶

    Amortised cost
    What a security or loan is carried at when it is not marked to market: the price paid, moved gradually towards the amount repaid at maturity. Changes in market rates do not change it.
    Fair value
    What a security would sell for today. When rates rise, the fair value of a fixed-rate bond falls.
    Unrealised gain or loss
    Fair value less amortised cost: what the bank would gain or lose if it sold the security today. It becomes realised only when the security is sold.
    Held to maturity (HTM)
    Securities the bank intends to keep until they mature. Carried at amortised cost, so their unrealised losses do not appear in the accounts or in capital. Most of SVB's losses were here.
    Available for sale (AFS)
    Securities the bank may sell. Carried at fair value; unrealised gains and losses go to equity, in AOCI, rather than to profit.
    Accumulated other comprehensive income (AOCI)
    The part of equity that holds unrealised gains and losses on AFS securities.
    FVOCI and FVTPL
    The IFRS 9 names for how a security is measured: at fair value through other comprehensive income (like AFS), or at fair value through profit or loss (a trading book).
    Net interest income (NII)
    Interest earned on assets less interest paid on deposits and borrowing: a bank's main source of profit.

    Capital¶

    Regulatory capital
    The equity a bank must hold to absorb losses, measured as regulators define it.
    Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1)
    The highest-quality capital: common equity less intangible assets such as goodwill. Under the US "AOCI filter", which smaller US banks could opt into, unrealised losses on AFS securities are left out of CET1; under Basel III they count.
    Tier 1 and total capital
    Tier 1 adds Additional Tier 1 (AT1) instruments, such as preferred stock, to CET1. Total capital adds Tier 2 capital, such as subordinated debt.
    Risk-weighted assets (RWA)
    The bank's exposures, each weighted by its riskiness: a Treasury bond weighs 0%, a corporate loan around 100%. The lab uses the Basel III standardised approach.
    Capital ratio
    Capital divided by RWA. The CET1 ratio must stay above 4.5%, plus buffers.
    Leverage ratio
    Tier 1 capital divided by total exposure, with no risk weights.

    Interest-rate risk¶

    Basis point (bp)
    One hundredth of a percentage point: +200bp means rates rise by 2 percentage points.
    Yield curve
    The interest rate for each maturity, from one month to thirty years. The lab uses US Treasury constant maturity (CMT) yields.
    Interest rate risk in the banking book (IRRBB)
    The risk that rate changes reduce a bank's value or income from the loans, securities and deposits it holds, rather than from trading.
    Economic value of equity (EVE)
    The present value of the bank's assets less that of its liabilities. ΔEVE is how much it changes under a rate shock.
    The six Basel shocks
    Standard rate scenarios from the Basel IRRBB standard: parallel up and down, steepener, flattener, and short rates up and down. See Scenarios.
    Duration
    How much a position's value changes when rates move: a duration of 5 years means it loses about 5% for each 1 percentage point rise in rates.
    Deposit beta
    The share of a rise in market rates that a bank passes on to depositors. A beta of 0.35 means a +100bp rise lifts the deposit rate by 35bp.
    Core deposits
    The stable part of a bank's non-maturity deposits, which in practice stays for years even though customers could withdraw it at any time.

    Data and the lab¶

    Call Report
    The quarterly financial report every US bank files with its regulators, published by the FDIC. The lab can build a bank from one. See Call Report mapping.
    Bank file
    A JSON file listing a bank's positions on a date, from which the lab builds a bank. See Bank file.
    Scenario
    The path of interest rates a run follows: actual historical rates or a shock. See Scenarios.
    Run
    One bank through one scenario, day by day, with its statements, capital and risk recorded for each day.
