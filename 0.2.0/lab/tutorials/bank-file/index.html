    Tutorial: build a bank from scratch¶

    Time: 30 minutes · You need: a text editor, and the browser app or the command line · You will learn: how to describe a balance sheet in a bank file, build it and run it.

    A bank file is a JSON document listing a bank's positions on one date (the as-of date), with their contract terms and book values. The lab turns it into a simulation: every security and loan is treated as bought on the as-of date at its book value, and equity is whatever balances the books.

    We will build Riverside Community Bank, a $1.6bn bank with Treasuries, agency MBS, municipal bonds, mortgages, business loans and consumer loans. The finished file is riverside.json.

    Step 1: the frame¶

    {
  "name": "Riverside Community Bank",
  "as_of": "2021-12-31",
  "cash": 60000000,
  "deposits": [],
  "positions": []
}
    • as_of is the balance-sheet date. The simulation starts the next day. Pick a business day.
    • Amounts are in currency units (here dollars). Use the same unit everywhere. If you work in thousands, add "unit": 1000 so the browser app shows dollars.
    • cash is cash and balances at other banks.

    Step 2: deposits¶

    "deposits": [
  {"id": "checking", "name": "Checking accounts", "balance": 680000000, "interest_rate": 0.0, "runoff_rate": 0.05,
   "core_share": 0.9, "core_maturity": 5},
  {"id": "savings", "name": "Savings and money market", "balance": 520000000, "interest_rate": 0.001, "beta": 0.3,
   "core_share": 0.7, "core_maturity": 4.5},
  {"id": "time", "name": "Time deposits", "balance": 230000000, "interest_rate": 0.008, "beta": 0.6}
]
    Field Meaning
    balance balance on the as-of date
    interest_rate rate paid on the as-of date (0.001 = 0.1%)
    beta share of each move in the 3-month Treasury yield passed on (0.3 = 30%); the rate never goes below zero
    runoff_rate share of the balance that leaves per year, continuously (0.05 = 5% a year)
    withdrawals one-off outflows: [["2023-03-09", 0.25]] takes 25% of the balance on 9 March 2023
    core_share, core_maturity for interest-rate risk: the share of the balance that is stable, and its average life in years. Here the Basel caps for retail checking (90%, 5 years) and savings (70%, 4.5 years); the time deposits get none

    Interest is accrued daily and paid on the first of each month.

    Step 3: securities¶

    Each position has an id, a type, its contract terms, an issuer, an accounting basis and its book values:

    {"id": "ust", "type": "treasury_note", "face_value": 150000000, "coupon_rate": 0.0125,
 "issue_date": "2021-11-30", "maturity_date": "2026-11-30",
 "issuer": {"name": "US Treasury", "issuer_type": "SOVEREIGN", "credit_rating": "AA_PLUS"},
 "basis": "FVOCI", "amortized_cost": 149500000, "fair_value": 149000000}
    • basis is the accounting category: AMORTIZED_COST (held to maturity), FVOCI (available for sale) or FVTPL (trading).
    • amortized_cost and fair_value are clean values (without accrued interest). FVOCI needs both; AMORTIZED_COST needs amortized_cost (and fair_value if you have it); FVTPL needs fair_value.
    • The lab solves each bond's effective interest rate from its amortised cost, and calibrates a credit spread so that its fair value on the as-of date is the one you gave.
    • issuer drives the credit risk weight: SOVEREIGN, PSE (agencies, municipalities), BANK, CORPORATE, INDIVIDUAL… with a credit_rating such as AA_PLUS.

    An agency mortgage pass-through and a municipal bond:

    {"id": "mbs", "type": "mortgage_backed_security", "face_value": 250000000, "coupon_rate": 0.02,
 "issue_date": "2021-12-31", "maturity": "28Y",
 "issuer": {"name": "Agency MBS", "issuer_type": "PSE", "credit_rating": "AA_PLUS"},
 "basis": "AMORTIZED_COST", "amortized_cost": 250000000, "fair_value": 248000000},
{"id": "muni", "type": "fixed_rate_bond", "face_value": 60000000, "coupon_rate": 0.025,
 "issue_date": "2020-06-01", "maturity_date": "2035-06-01",
 "issuer": {"name": "State and municipal", "issuer_type": "PSE", "credit_rating": "AA"},
 "basis": "AMORTIZED_COST", "amortized_cost": 61000000, "fair_value": 62000000}

    The MBS pool's prepayments speed up when mortgage rates fall and slow when they rise; see the bank file reference.

    Step 4: loans¶

    Loans are held at amortised cost and must be on the books at par (amortised cost = face value outstanding):

    {"id": "mortgages", "type": "residential_mortgage", "face_value": 500000000, "interest_rate": 0.031,
 "issue_date": "2021-12-31", "maturity": "30Y", "ltv": 0.7,
 "issuer": {"name": "Households", "issuer_type": "INDIVIDUAL"},
 "basis": "AMORTIZED_COST", "amortized_cost": 500000000},
{"id": "business", "type": "variable_rate_loan", "face_value": 400000000, "spread": 0.015,
 "issue_date": "2021-12-31", "maturity": "5Y", "benchmark_family": "prime",
 "repricing_frequency": "1M", "payment_frequency": "1M", "principal_repayment_mode": "bullet",
 "issuer": {"name": "Local businesses", "issuer_type": "CORPORATE"},
 "basis": "AMORTIZED_COST", "amortized_cost": 400000000},
{"id": "consumer", "type": "amortizing_fixed_rate_loan", "face_value": 120000000, "interest_rate": 0.045,
 "issue_date": "2021-12-31", "maturity": "5Y", "frequency": "1M",
 "issuer": {"name": "Consumers", "issuer_type": "INDIVIDUAL"},
 "basis": "AMORTIZED_COST", "amortized_cost": 120000000}
    • A mortgage needs ltv (loan-to-value, 0.7 = 70%) for its risk weight.
    • The business loan pays the Prime rate plus 1.5%, resetting monthly.
    • Periods are written "30Y", "5Y", "1M".

    Step 5: everything else¶

    "other_assets": 30000000,
"other_liabilities": 15000000,
"operating": {"noninterest_expense": 28000000, "noninterest_income": 6000000, "tax_rate": 0.21, "dividend_payout": 0.3},
"regulatory": {"aoci_filter": true, "operational_rwa": 60000000}
    • other_assets (premises, goodwill, other) and other_liabilities are carried unchanged. Other assets are risk-weighted at 100%.
    • operating gives the annual non-interest expense (salaries, premises, technology) and income (fees), the tax rate, and the share of profit after tax paid out as dividends. Without it, the bank has no costs and pays no tax.
    • regulatory: whether AOCI is excluded from CET1, and the operational-risk RWA (the lab does not derive it from a bank file). additional_tier1 and tier2 can also be given.

    Step 6: build and run it¶

    1. Start brms and open Import.
    2. Drop riverside.json on From a bank file (or paste it), then click Build from bank file.
    3. On the bank's page, click Run all Basel shocks.
    python -m brms.lab build-bank riverside.json --out riverside.zip
python -m brms.lab run riverside.zip --scenario historical --scenario parallel+200 \
    --scenario steepener --months 12 --out results

    results/historical/, results/parallel_up/ and results/steepener/ then hold metrics.csv (every metric, every market day), carrying_value.csv, fair_value.csv and summary.json.

    Output of the command line:

    scenario            CET1 end  CET1 min   dEVE/T1     net income
historical            18.01%    15.89%   -70.42%     18,091,207
parallel_up           18.13%    15.88%   -44.70%     19,417,565
steepener             17.73%    15.89%   -52.28%     15,047,752

    Riverside starts with a CET1 ratio of 15.9% and builds capital through the year. Its economic value is still very exposed: 30-year mortgages and 28-year MBS funded by deposits, of which only the core part offsets the loss. The worst ΔEVE (−70%) is on the first day of the historical run, at the low rates of January 2022, when long fixed-rate assets are most sensitive. Under the +200bp shock the bank starts from higher rates, so the same Basel shocks cost less (−45%). By the end of 2022 both runs are at about −35%. See assumptions.

    When something is wrong¶

    The builder checks the file and stops with a message naming the position:

    Message Fix
    Position 'x' (FVOCI) needs 'fair_value' Add the missing value.
    loans bought at a premium or discount are not supported yet Make the loan's amortized_cost equal its face_value.
    The bank file implies non-positive equity Assets must exceed deposits and other liabilities.
    cannot be risk-weighted: the mortgage has no loan-to-value ratio Add ltv to mortgages.
    Unknown prepayment model fields / Unknown operating fields Check the spelling against the reference.
