    Tutorial: SVB through 2022¶

    Time: 15 minutes · You need: the browser app (brms) · You will learn: how unrealised losses build up when rates rise, why they did not show in SVB's regulatory capital, and what changes when they count.

    The story¶

    At the end of 2021 Silicon Valley Bank had $209bn of assets. Deposits had poured in during 2021 and SVB had invested most of them in long-dated, fixed-rate securities: Treasuries and agency mortgage-backed securities (MBS) yielding about 1.5%. In 2022 the Federal Reserve raised rates from near zero to over 4%. Fixed-rate securities lose value when rates rise. In March 2023 depositors ran, SVB sold securities at a loss to pay them, and it failed within two days.

    You will now replay 2022 on SVB's actual end-2021 balance sheet.

    Step 1: open the bank¶

    Start the app (brms), click Banks, then Silicon Valley Bank, end-2021.

    Note three numbers on the tiles:

    • Total assets $208.7bn.
    • Equity $14.9bn: everything the shareholders can lose before depositors lose.
    • AOCI filter: On. Keep this in mind for Step 4.

    Scroll to Positions and sort by Carrying value. The biggest holdings after the floating loans are MBS pools (1.52% 2046/12/31 MBS) and bonds (1.52% 2028/1/1 Fixed Rate Bond), held at Amortised cost (held to maturity, HTM) or FVOCI (available for sale, AFS).

    HTM and AFS

    A held-to-maturity security is carried at amortised cost: its price changes never reach the balance sheet. An available-for-sale security (FVOCI under IFRS 9) is carried at fair value, and its price changes go to accumulated other comprehensive income (AOCI), a part of equity, not to profit.

    Step 2: run 2022¶

    In Run a scenario: Historical rates, 12 months, AOCI filter: As the bank (on). Click Run scenario.

    Step 3: read what happened¶

    Measure 1 Jan 2022 30 Dec 2022
    10-year Treasury yield 1.5% 3.9%
    HTM unrealised loss about −$1bn −$12.7bn (worst: −$14.7bn on 20 Oct)
    AFS unrealised loss about −$0.1bn −$3.4bn
    Net income for the year +$2.2bn
    CET1 ratio 15.7% 19.2%

    Look at the Unrealised gains and losses chart: the HTM line falls with every rise in the 10-year yield on the Treasury yields chart.

    By October the losses on securities alone (about −$18bn) were larger than SVB's entire equity ($14.9bn). If SVB had had to sell everything, it would have been insolvent. Yet its CET1 ratio rose, from 15.7% to 19.2%, because:

    1. HTM losses are not in the books at all (amortised cost);
    2. AFS losses are in AOCI, but the AOCI filter removes AOCI from CET1, an option US banks below the largest size could take;
    3. the bank kept earning: $2.2bn of profit after costs and tax adds to capital.

    Open Books and drag the slider to 20 October 2022. In Statements, Accumulated OCI shows the AFS loss inside equity. The HTM loss appears nowhere on the balance sheet.

    Step 4: count the AFS losses in capital¶

    Under the full Basel III standard, AOCI counts in CET1. Go back to the bank, and in Run a scenario set:

    • AOCI filter: Off
    • Label: Historical, AOCI counted

    Run it. Now:

    AOCI filter on AOCI counted
    CET1 ratio at start 15.7% 15.4%
    Lowest CET1 ratio 15.7% (on day 1) 13.7% (14 Jun 2022)
    CET1 ratio at end 19.2% 15.3%

    With AOCI counted, capital falls through the first half of 2022 as AFS losses mount, then recovers slowly as profits accumulate. Even then, the much larger HTM loss is still invisible.

    Step 5: compare the two runs¶

    Open Runs, tick both runs and click Compare 2 selected. The CET1 ratio chart shows the two paths side by side; the summary table gives their lowest points.

    What you have learned¶

    • Rising rates cut the value of fixed-rate securities. For SVB, the 2022 losses on securities were larger than all its equity.
    • Accounting choices (HTM, AFS with the AOCI filter) kept those losses out of regulatory capital, so the CET1 ratio gave no warning.
    • The losses become real only when securities are sold, which is what a deposit run forces. SVB's last week plays that out.

    Try next¶

    • Run the Parallel up (+200bp) shock instead of historical rates. How does a sudden 200bp move compare with the gradual rise of 2022?
    • Open Silicon Valley Bank, end-2022 and run 3 months of historical rates. This is the bank the market was looking at in early 2023.
    • Read the Basel shocks tutorial to compare SVB with other banks.

    Why the lab's numbers differ from SVB's own

    The lab holds the end-2021 balance sheet fixed: it does not replay SVB's 2022 purchases, sales or deposit outflows, and it rebuilds the securities from the Call Report's maturity buckets. SVB reported HTM unrealised losses of $15.2bn (−16.6% of amortised cost) at the end of 2022; the lab gets −$12.7bn (−13%). See Validation.
